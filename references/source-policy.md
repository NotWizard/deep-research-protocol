# Source and evidence policy

Assess a source in relation to the claim it supports. Reputation alone does not make a source fit for every claim.

## Source classes

- `primary`: original data, regulatory filings, laws, standards, papers, transcripts, official records, and direct observations.
- `authoritative_secondary`: institutions or reporting with direct access, transparent methods, and independent analysis.
- `reliable_secondary`: established reporting or analysis that identifies its evidence.
- `aggregator`: useful for discovery but dependent on upstream material.
- `lead_only`: social posts, forums, search snippets, unsourced summaries, and prior AI reports.

`lead_only` material may generate queries but may not independently support a critical claim.

## Independence

Every admitted source has an `independence_group`. Sources belong to the same group when they share the material evidence or attribution chain, including syndicated copies of one article, articles repeating the same press release, databases derived from the same filing, and multiple company channels repeating one management statement.

Two URLs do not establish corroboration.

## Evidence admission

Admit evidence only when the source content was actually opened or supplied, the capture preserves enough text and location to review it, the evidence is atomic and source-faithful, limitations and qualifiers are retained, and its relation to a question or claim is explicit.

Do not convert a model summary into a quotation. Do not reconstruct text hidden by a paywall or truncated tool result.

## Claim standards

Separate actual results from guidance, historical observations from estimates, estimates from forecasts, company assertions from independently verified facts, correlation from causal inference, and absence of evidence from evidence of absence.

A critical supported claim needs either direct primary evidence appropriate to that claim or at least two high-quality sources from independent groups. This deterministic threshold does not prove entailment; the Verifier must still judge whether the evidence really supports the claim.

## Contradictions

Do not choose a preferred source merely to make the narrative cleaner. Investigate whether disagreement comes from time window, definition, sample, unit or currency, actual versus forecast, gross versus net measurement, source incentives, or genuine factual conflict.

If unresolved, preserve both sides and specify how the report will present the uncertainty.
